= feature/braids: braid words, closure to PD codes and the closure invariants

Tracks the braid kernel behind `src/braid.jl`: TANGLE-style braid words,
closing a braid into a planar diagram in the KnotAtlas PD convention, and
checking that the closure really is the link it should be.

Minimal working prototype: `examples/prototypes/braids.jl`.
Self-contained (standard library only, no KnotKnot import), exits non-zero
unless every pinned assertion holds.

== Conventions pinned by the prototype

Generators are `(i, sign)` pairs: `sigma_i` has sign `+1` (the strand at
position `i` passes OVER the strand at `i+1`), `sigma_i^-1` has sign `-1`.
Parsing is TANGLE-style: `s1.s1.s1` for positive generators, `S2` for
inverses, whitespace tolerated, empty tokens skipped.

Closure connects bottom strand `k` back to top strand `k`. A positive
generator closes to `X[in_(i+1), in_i, out_i, out_(i+1)]`; a negative one to
`X[in_i, out_i, out_(i+1), in_(i+1)]`. The empty braid closes to the unknot.

Three independent checks must agree, and they are what make this more than a
parser test:

. **Components vs permutation.** The link components of the closure PD —
   cycles of the through-strand pairing `a<->c`, `d<->b` — equal the cycles of
   the permutation the braid induces on its strands. Asserted for ten words.
. **Determinant.** Computed exactly from the Fox colouring matrix of the
   closure: rows are crossings, columns are arcs, `+2` on the over arc and
   `-1` on each under arc, then the absolute determinant of the minor that
   deletes the last row and column. A one-crossing knot has an empty minor,
   whose determinant is 1.
. **Genus.** From the Seifert circles of the closure, matching `(k-1)/2` for
   the `(2,k)`-torus knots.

Also asserted: the closure of a knot braid has exactly as many arcs as
crossings; mirroring the word (flipping every sign) preserves the
determinant; and the conjugate half-twists `s1.s2.s1` and `s2.s1.s2` both
close to the Hopf link, like `s1.s1`.

== Risks to guard against

* **Arc labels are not arcs.** As in the Alexander and quandle kernels, the
  over strand of `X[a,b,c,d]` joins `d` to `b`; those two PD labels must be
  merged before the colouring matrix is built. Using the `2c` labels as
  columns makes the minor identically zero, so every determinant comes out 0
  and the failure looks like a braid bug rather than a labelling bug.
* **The closure renaming.** The bottom-to-top relabelling is what makes the
  closure a diagram at all; skipping it leaves dangling arcs and the
  component count silently disagrees with the permutation cycles. The
  permutation check is the guard.
* **Redundant words.** `s1.s1.S1` closes to the unknot with a kink: three
  crossings, determinant 1, but writhe `+1`. Diagram invariants (writhe,
  crossing count) are NOT link invariants, and tests must not expect them to
  be.
* **Links vs knots.** The genus formula `(c - s + 1)/2` is only integral for
  knots; the prototype pins genus for knots only.
* **Determinant is an obstruction.** `s1.s2.s1` and `s1.s1` both give the Hopf
  link, and `4_1` and `5_1` share determinant 5. Do not treat the determinant
  as an identification.

== Plan going forward

. **Braid group reductions.** Implement the braid relations
   (`sigma_i sigma_j = sigma_j sigma_i` for `|i-j| > 1`,
   `sigma_i sigma_{i+1} sigma_i = sigma_{i+1} sigma_i sigma_{i+1}`) so that a
   word can be reduced, and check that reduction preserves the closure
   invariants.
. **Markov moves.** Stabilisation and destabilisation, and a test that
   Markov-equivalent words give the same link invariants.
. **Feeding the other kernels.** Route the closure PD through the Alexander,
   Jones, Seifert and quandle prototypes, and pin the full invariant vector
   for each word in the table above.
. **Braid index.** Compute the minimal braid index for the knots in the table
   and check it against the known values (2 for `3_1`, `4_1`, `5_1`).
. **Alexander's theorem in practice.** Given a PD code, find a braid word
   whose closure it is (Yamada's algorithm or Vogel's), and round-trip
   `PD -> braid -> PD`.
. **Interop.** Emit braid words in the shapes KnotTheory.jl and Skein.jl
   expect, and accept them back.

== Acceptance criteria

* `julia examples/prototypes/braids.jl` exits 0.
* Every table entry above is reproduced exactly.
* The component/permutation agreement holds for every tested word.
* No KnotKnot import in the prototype.
